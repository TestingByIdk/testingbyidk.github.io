🎤 Interview Cheat Sheet

This is to answer interview questions ahead of time.

What is your biggest weakness?

Honestly, remembering names and faces. I can remember conversations, tasks, and things people tell me, but sometimes it takes me a bit longer to match names to faces. Once I've worked with someone for a little while though, it's usually not a problem.

Why should we hire you?

I think I bring a mix of skills that you don't usually find all in one person. I've worked in leadership roles, customer service, technical support, food service, and social media management. More importantly, I care about doing a good job and helping my team succeed. If I'm given the opportunity, I'll work hard to prove I was worth hiring.

Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

I want to be leading a team that I can trust and rely on. I'd like to be managing websites, helping projects grow, and continuing to build my skills. Most importantly, I want to enjoy what I do and work with people who enjoy what they do too.

What is your availability?

I'm currently available Monday through Sunday, open to close. I'm flexible and can work different schedules, but I'm mainly looking for a full-time position where I can grow, contribute, and become a long-term part of the team. A consistent schedule would be nice, but I'm willing to work around business needs.

Are you looking for full-time or part-time work?

I'm mainly looking for full-time work. I enjoy being part of a team and taking on responsibility, and I feel like I can contribute more when I'm working consistently. My goal is to build a long-term career, not just pick up a few shifts here and there.

Describe a conflict you had to overcome.

I've dealt with quite a few conflicts while working in leadership roles. Usually it involved a customer or coworker who was having a rough day. With customers, I always tried to understand the problem and fix it before it became a bigger issue. Sometimes that meant correcting a mistake or offering something small to make things right. With coworkers, I tried to listen to what was bothering them and find a solution that worked for everyone. A lot of problems can be solved when people feel heard and respected.

Describe a mistake you made at work and how you handled it.

I've made mistakes before because I'm human. What matters is how you respond to them. When I worked as a cook, there were times I made a dish incorrectly. Instead of blaming someone else or hoping nobody noticed, I fixed it right away and made sure it went out properly. I've always believed that owning a mistake and correcting it is better than trying to avoid responsibility.
